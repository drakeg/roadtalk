# Commercialization

RoadTalk is being developed as a potential revenue-generating product, but no customer demand, revenue, or willingness to pay is claimed yet.

## SMB customer hypotheses

The first validation targets should be small organizations where location-aware voice coordination can replace radio friction or fragmented phone/chat coordination:

1. **Campgrounds and RV parks** — staff coordination, guest assistance, maintenance, events, and geographically dispersed operations.
2. **Guided-tour and outdoor operators** — guides and support staff coordinating moving groups.
3. **Small event, convoy, or field-service teams** — short-lived teams that need push-to-talk and location context without purchasing dedicated radio infrastructure.

These are hypotheses to test, not validated market segments.

## Value proposition hypothesis

RoadTalk may save staff time and reduce coordination friction by combining push-to-talk, channels, route/location context, and mobile access in one experience while retaining explicit privacy controls.

## Fast validation path

After Sprint 16 readiness:
- conduct 5–10 interviews in one selected segment;
- demonstrate only the bounded MVP workflow relevant to that segment;
- ask for a paid pilot rather than collecting generic interest;
- define pilot success with measurable time saved, successful coordination tasks, reliability, adoption, and willingness to renew;
- avoid adding features that do not improve the pilot's buying decision.

## Pricing hypothesis

Do not treat pricing as validated. Start testing a simple per-site or per-team monthly subscription plus optional onboarding only after interviews establish the buyer and measurable value. Record actual objections and willingness-to-pay evidence before changing the roadmap.

## Product boundary

Commercialization never overrides RoadTalk's security, privacy, production-authorization, provider, or spend gates. A demo/pilot must use an approved environment and cannot silently activate paid services.
